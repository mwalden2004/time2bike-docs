Your public pages are built to be found by Google, and by whoever your riders
share the link with. You don't have to configure any of it, but a few of your
own choices make a real difference.

## What happens automatically

- Your event, organization, and series pages are submitted to search engines
  for you.
- Each page gets a title and summary built from what you've already filled in.
- Sharing a link on social media pulls in your banner image, event name, and
  description automatically.
- Draft and hidden events stay invisible. They're never indexed, and neither is
  anything behind a login.
- **Unlisted** events are reachable by link but kept out of listings, search
  results, and search engines are told not to list them. That's the whole
  point of the status. Use it for a soft launch, then switch to **Published**
  when you want to be found.
  → [Event settings](/organizers/events/event-settings)

## What actually moves the needle

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Write a real description" icon="align-left">
    Put your event name, discipline, city, and state in the first sentence of
    your event description. That's what people search for ("gravel race
    Bentonville") and it's what search engines read first.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Upload a good banner" icon="image">
    Your banner is the preview image whenever anyone shares your event on social
    media. A sharp, bright action shot gets clicked; a stretched logo doesn't.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Use one link everywhere" icon="link">
    Put the same `time2.bike/events/your-event` link in every post, email, and
    flyer. Splitting your traffic across different links weakens all of them.

    And don't change your web address once you've publicised it. Old links
    don't redirect. → [Web addresses](/platform/slugs)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Get shared early" icon="share-nodes">
    Links from local clubs, shops, and tourism sites are worth more than
    anything you can change on the page. Ask the venue and your sponsors to link
    to your event page rather than to a PDF flyer.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Knowing what worked

Time2Bike remembers where each registration came from. If you tag your own links
(the ones in your newsletter, your Instagram bio, a partner's site) you'll see
which channel produced which signups under **Event → Analytics**, in the
**Attribution** section.

Any tracking tags you add to a link are carried through to the registration, so
whatever you already use for campaign tracking keeps working.

→ [Event analytics](/organizers/events/event-analytics)

## Rider profiles

Riders' public profiles are findable too, which is how a rider's results turn up
when someone searches their name. Riders control this themselves under
**Account → Privacy**.

→ [Privacy](/riders/account/privacy)
