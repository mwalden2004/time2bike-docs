This is the full day, in the order it happens. It follows the USAC dual slalom
format used at collegiate conference races: mixed-category qualifying, a bracket
per category, head-to-head with lane swaps and a maximum differential.

If you just need the buttons, see [Dual slalom](/timing/formats/dual-slalom) for
the operator screen and [Brackets](/timing/formats/brackets) for the bracket
panel.

## How the day is shaped

<Steps>
  <Step title="Practice and course inspection">
    Nothing to time. Riders session both lanes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Qualifying">
    Every rider takes a timed run: **one run, down one lane**. All categories
    go through the chute together, first come first served. The lane is the
    same for everyone in a category, which is what makes the times comparable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Seeding">
    Fastest qualifier is the top seed in their category. You decide how many
    advance. 8, 16, whatever the field and the daylight support.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Head-to-head rounds">
    Two riders, red and blue. Two runs with the lanes swapped so both ride both
    courses. Lowest combined time wins the match, capped by the maximum
    differential. Winner advances.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Finals">
    Semifinal losers usually ride off for third, so the sheet places 1 to 4.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Results">
    Finalize, publish, print.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Before the day

**Make one race per category** (Men's A, Men's B, Women's A, and so on) and
set each one's format to **dual slalom bracket** on the
[Races](/organizers/races/races-list) page. That's what gives them the two-lane
screen and their own bracket.

Assign bib numbers as usual. Everything else is set up on the day.

## Qualifying several categories at once

This is the part that would be painful if you had to do it per category, so it
isn't. Turn on **seeding mode** on the timing screen and you can time riders
from *any* dual slalom race in the event through both lanes at once: a men's A
rider in red, a women's B rider in blue, in whatever order they present at the
gate.

**Each rider's time records against their own category**, so the brackets can
seed from it afterwards. You never sort riders into categories at the gate.

{/* IMAGE NEEDED: Seeding mode with two riders from different categories staged in red and blue */}

### One lane per field

A field qualifies down **one** lane. The screen works out which lane it's using
from the first rider down it, then flags anyone who runs the other one, because
a time set on the other course isn't comparable, and silently ranking it against
everybody else's is how you seed a bracket wrong.

The qualifying board shows the lane, who still owes a run, and anyone who went
down the wrong side. If you've
[combined categories](#running-several-categories-as-one-field), the board
covers the whole combined field — that's who is being compared.

<Note>
  **Both lanes is a nationals thing.** At nationals riders qualify down both
  lanes and the seed time is red + blue combined, which cancels out any
  difference between the courses. Bracket setup offers that as an option, but
  for a conference race one run each is all qualifying needs.
</Note>

### If somebody needs a re-run

Just run them again. The best time in a lane is what counts, so a second run
only helps them.

## Running several categories as one field

A dual-slalom category rarely fills a bracket on its own. Six women's Cat 3
riders don't make a bracket worth running, and neither do four men's P1. So
categories get combined on the morning, off the entry sheet — the women run as
one field, the men split P1 off and run Cat 1/2/3 together.

Press **Combine categories** on the dual-slalom operator screen (it reads
**Combined fields** once you have some).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Make a field and name it">
    "Women", "Men Cat 1/2/3" — whatever you'll call it over the PA. The name is
    what the bracket, the running order and the printed sheet all use.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap the categories that run in it">
    Each shows its entry count, so you can see the field is worth running before
    you commit. A category already in another field is greyed out — a race lives
    in one field or none.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose how places are awarded">
    **A podium per category** — they race together, and each category is placed
    1st, 2nd, 3rd among its own riders. This is what a rider who entered Cat 3
    expects to see.

    **One podium** — one field, one set of places, and every rider keeps the
    place they took in the bracket.
  </Step>
</Steps>

{/* IMAGE NEEDED: The Combined fields modal with two fields built and the podium choice visible */}

Riders stay entered in their own category throughout. A combined field changes
who they qualify against and who they race — never what they're scored as, which
licence they're filed under, or which league points they earn. Every run is
recorded against the rider's own race.

Anything you don't combine keeps running on its own; the modal lists those under
**Running on their own** so you can see what's left out.

<Warning>
  Combining a category that already has decided matches sets its bracket aside.
  Nothing is deleted — separating the categories again brings it back with its
  results — but the new combined field starts from an empty bracket. The modal
  says so before you save.
</Warning>

<Note>
  If someone else changed the grouping on another device while you had the modal
  open, you're told. Saving replaces theirs with what's on your screen; close and
  reopen to start from their version instead.
</Note>

## Setting up each category's bracket

Once a field is timed, switch to it and open **Setup bracket**. A combined field
gets **one** bracket covering all its categories; anything running on its own
gets its own, as before.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick the bracket format">
    **Single + 3rd place** is the collegiate default: a straight knockout plus
    a ride-off between the semifinal losers, so the sheet places 1 through 4.

    The others are single elimination (1st and 2nd only), single + consolation
    (places every first-round loser, 5th–8th in a field of eight, at the cost of
    more runs),
    and double elimination.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the match format">
    **Combined time** is the standard: both runs added, lowest total wins.

    Best of 2 scores by runs won. Single run is one and done, for when you're
    losing the light.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say how many riders advance">
    This is your real decision: *31 qualified, 16 advance*. The bracket itself
    is the next power of two above that, and the top seeds take byes for the
    difference.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Seed from qualifying">
    Choose **From quali times** and the order fills in fastest-first. You can
    still drag riders around, or seed manually or at random.

    The dialog tells you how many riders have a qualifying time, so you can see
    at a glance whether anybody is missing before you build.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the maximum differential">
    Defaults to **1.5 seconds**, per USAC 5E2(c). Adjust it if your course
    warrants.
  </Step>
</Steps>

{/* IMAGE NEEDED: Bracket setup with "From quali times" chosen and the seed order filled in */}

Repeat per field. Each one gets its own bracket, its own seeds, and its own
podium — or a podium per category inside it, if that's how you set the combined
field up.

### What the maximum differential does

If a rider wins the first run by more than the maximum, only the maximum is
carried into the second run. A blown corner costs you the run, not the match, so
a rider who goes down on run one can still ride back into it.

The same cap applies when someone doesn't finish a run at all: they're charged
the maximum rather than an infinite gap.

The match panel shows each rider's carried time, and marks it when it has hit
the cap.

## Running the rounds

Riders swap lanes between the two runs, and the screen tracks which lane each
one is in. The starter stages, and the finishers press their own lane's button.

**Running order** sits above the on-deck list, and there are two ways to work
a round:

- **Pair runs both** (default). A pair goes, swaps lanes, goes again, and the
  match is decided before the next pair is called. This is how the paper score
  sheet is laid out: each match resolves to its "won by" in place. It also
  keeps run numbers in order, because a match's two runs are numbered
  consecutively.
- **All leg 1 first** — every matchup runs once, then the whole round goes
  again with the lanes swapped. Keeps the start hut moving when the course
  takes a while to reset.

Pick it at bracket setup, or flip it from the operator screen mid-round. It
is stored on the bracket, so both lane operators are always called to the same
pair. It changes nothing about the bracket itself, only the order the screen
offers matchups in, so switching costs nothing and you can always tap a match
in the bracket to jump straight to it.

Full mechanics: [Dual slalom](/timing/formats/dual-slalom).

Byes advance automatically when you **Resolve byes**, which is worth pressing
after setup and after any substitution.

<Tip>
  Put one person on each lane's finish and one at the gate, and nobody has more
  than one button. It's the difference between a smooth bracket and a protest.
</Tip>

### Re-running a heat, or fixing one result

Click any run to open that heat on its own. Use the **Fix / re-run…** button on
the result banner, a row in **Recent finishes**, or the run number in the run
times sheet.

From there you can:

- **Correct a time** for either lane.
- **Charge a penalty** (jump start, missed gate) on one rider. That rider is
  charged the maximum differential for the run and cannot win it, per USAC
  5E2(c).
- **Re-run the heat.** Both times are cleared and the pair goes back into the
  running order *at the same run number*, so the re-run replaces the original
  instead of sitting beside it. Use this when they are going again.
- **Delete the run** outright, so the match scores as if that heat never
  happened. Needs marshal rights.

Every one of these is written to the session log and noted on the result, so a
protest can be walked back afterwards.

<Tip>
  Re-run and delete are not the same thing. Re-run keeps the slot and expects
  a new time; delete removes the heat from the match. If a rider is about to
  drop in again, you want re-run.
</Tip>

### When it goes wrong

Slalom generates more corrections than any other format. All of these are
available from the match panel:

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Wrong rider in a match | **Edit match** → substitute by plate or name |
| Riders on the wrong sides | **Edit match** → swap A and B |
| A leg time is wrong | Fix it inline, or **Edit all times** for a table of every leg |
| A rider can't take their run | **Forfeit / DNF** — the opponent advances |
| Seeding was wrong | **Edit seeds**, available until a match is decided |
| The bracket looks broken | **Resolve byes** first; then **Edit all times** to see what's missing |

→ [Brackets](/timing/formats/brackets)

## Finishing and publishing

<Steps>
  <Step title="Finish every field's bracket">
    Including the third-place ride-off, if you're running one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the placings while riders are still there">
    A disputed match is much easier to settle with both riders in front of you
    than by email on Tuesday.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Finalize">
    Locks the results. It won't let you through while any device still has
    times waiting to upload, which is exactly the check you want here.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish">
    Results go live on the event page, land on each rider's profile, and
    finishers get an email.

    → [Finalize & publish](/timing/results/finalize-and-publish)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Print for the awards tent">
    **Results PDF**, or the combined event PDF for every category on one sheet.

    → [Exports](/timing/results/exports)
  </Step>
</Steps>

Published results show the bracket diagram alongside the finishing order, so
riders can see the path each rider took rather than a bare list of placings.

scoring is applied by whoever runs your league from those placings. See the

## Worth knowing before your first one

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Set the displayed precision to hundredths" icon="stopwatch">
    Slalom margins are routinely under a second, and whole seconds will show you
    ties that aren't. **Timer setup → How I am timing → Show times to → 0.01s**.

    Every time is stored to the millisecond regardless, so you can tighten it
    afterwards if two riders still tie.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Set pause to freeze the clock" icon="pause">
    A course hold shouldn't count against anyone. **Timer setup → How I am
    timing → What pause means**. It locks once the first time is recorded, so do
    it before qualifying.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Combined finish isn't available, and doesn't need to be" icon="layer-group">
    A dual slalom race always keeps its two-lane screen. If your event has other
    races as well, they can still use a shared chute. The two coexist.

    → [Combined finish](/timing/formats/combined-finish)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="An RFID pad will resolve lane finishes for you" icon="tower-broadcast">
    While a run is in flight, a read for a plate that started in red records a
    red-lane finish. The **Listening for plates** panel shows which two bibs the
    watcher is waiting for.

    Watch for a pad near the start line firing on warm-up runs.

    → [RFID](/timing/hardware/rfid)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Beam timing pays for itself here" icon="bolt">
    Two riders separated by hundredths is exactly where human reaction time at
    the button stops being good enough.

    → [Laser timing](/timing/hardware/laser)
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
