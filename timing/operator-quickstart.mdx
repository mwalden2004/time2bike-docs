You've been handed a phone and asked to time a race. This is everything you need
and nothing you don't.

## 1. Get on the timing screen

**If the race director sent you a QR code or a link**, scan or open it on the
phone you'll be timing with. You'll see a card confirming which event and race
you're joining — tap **Continue**. That's it. No password, no account.

The link remembers itself on that phone, so if the screen closes you can just
open the browser again.

![Join page](/images/timing/join-op.png)

**If you have a Time2Bike login for this organization**, sign in and open
**Event → Live Timing**, then tap your session.

## 2. Check you're on the right race

Across the top is a strip of race tabs. **Whichever one is highlighted is where
your finishes go.** If the event has one race, ignore this. If it has several,
check it before every single race.

![Live Timing](/images/timing/home.png)

## 3. Start the clock

Press the big **Start** button when the race begins.

Missed it? Don't panic — record finishes anyway. They'll say "awaiting start"
until someone presses Start, then the times work themselves out.

## 4. Record finishes

Three ways, use whichever is fastest in the moment:

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Type the bib" icon="keyboard">
    Type the number into the big input and press **Enter**. Fastest by a mile.
    The clock keeps running — go as fast as you can type.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Tap a name" icon="hand-pointer">
    The **Registered riders** list shows everyone in this race. Tap a name to
    record them finishing right now. Use it when a bib is unreadable or covered
    in mud.
  </Card>
</Columns>

![XC finish](/images/timing/xc-finish.png)

**Two-lane racing (dual slalom)?** Your screen is one giant **FINISH** button
for your lane. Press it when your rider crosses. That's your whole job.
→ [Dual slalom](/timing/operating/dual-slalom)

## 5. Fix mistakes

Long-press (or right-click) any rider's row for the correction menu:

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Set manual time** | Type in a time you took by hand |
| **DNF / DNS / DSQ** | Didn't finish / didn't start / disqualified |
| **Add note** | Anything the director should see later |
| **Delete** | Removes a wrong entry from the results |

Nothing you do here is permanent damage — the original entry is always
recoverable, and a director can undo anything.

![Result actions](/images/timing/result-actions.png)

## 6. About signal

**You don't need it.** Every finish is saved to your phone the moment you record
it. If you're in a canyon with no bars, keep timing exactly as normal.

The dot in the corner tells you where things stand:

| | Meaning | Do you need to do anything? |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 | Everything uploaded | No |
| 🟡 | A few finishes waiting to upload | No — they'll go when there's signal |
| 🔴 | No signal, recording to this phone | No — keep timing |

<Warning>
  If the dot isn't green, the finishes on this phone haven't reached anyone else
  yet. **Don't put the phone away, hand it off, or let it die** until it goes
  green. Walk it to a spot with signal if you have to.
</Warning>

## 7. When the race ends

1. Press **Stop** on the race.
2. Look down the finish list. Anything the screen has flagged, sort out now
   while riders are still around.
3. Mark anyone who didn't start as **DNS**.
4. Hand the phone back, or if you have the access: **Finalize**, then
   **Publish**.

## Cheat sheet

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Which race am I recording into | Tab strip across the top |
| Type a bib | Big input under the tabs |
| Riders still out on course | "On course" list |
| Tap someone in instead of typing | "Registered riders" list |
| Fix a finish | Long-press the row |
| Have my finishes uploaded? | Coloured dot, top right |
| Refresh | Pull down on the page |
| Lock the results | **Finalize**, at the bottom |
| Put results on the website | **Publish**, after finalize |

## If something looks wrong

Take a screenshot and tell the race director. Nothing you can press loses data —
every entry, every edit, and every deletion is recorded, so a problem can always
be unpicked afterwards.
