Time2Bike records a timestamp for each timing action and calculates elapsed
times, places, and gaps from the recorded starts and crossings. A button press
records when the device processes the action; it does not independently verify
when the rider crossed the line.

## Correcting a start

You can record finishes before entering a race start. Those results wait for a
start time before an elapsed time can be calculated.

If the start button was missed or pressed late, use **Set the start time** or
**Correct the start time** and enter the start from your backup record. Pressing
**START RACE** late records the current time, not the earlier gun. Review the
recalculated results after making the correction.

## Comparing devices

While connected, each device periodically estimates its clock offset against
Time2Bike's server. Recorded actions use the offset available at that moment.
Network delay, device clock changes, and time spent offline can affect the
estimate.

The top bar shows **Clock OK**, **Syncing clock…**, or **Clock rough**. On a
computer, hover over the indicator for the estimated uncertainty in milliseconds.
This is a clock-sync indicator, not a certification of finish accuracy or a
measurement of an operator's reaction time.

## Recording while offline

A prepared device can queue timing actions locally while disconnected. On
reconnection, it uploads the stored timestamps rather than treating the upload
time as the crossing time. Check for queued actions, sync errors, and conflicting
corrections before relying on the combined results.

Retries of the same saved action are recognized by their identifier. Separate
recordings of the same rider are different actions and can need duplicate review.
See [Timing without signal](/timing/hardware/offline-and-sync).

## Display precision

Timing values are recorded in milliseconds. Choose how many digits to show under
**Timer setup → How I am timing**:

| Setting | Shows |
|---|---|
| Thousandths | `12:34.567` |
| Hundredths | `12:34.56` |
| Tenths | `12:34.5` |
| Whole seconds | `12:34` |

Changing the display setting does not improve the accuracy of the measurement.
Extra digits can reveal a recorded difference, but they do not establish that
one rider finished ahead of another. Choose the display and timing method to
match your event's rules and required accuracy.

## If the clock indicator looks bad

- Check the device's date and time are set to update automatically.
- Improve the connection and allow another clock-sync sample to complete.
- Review timestamps captured while the clock was uncertain against your backup.
- Use a tested backup timing method if the uncertainty could affect placings.

Even a small timing difference can matter in any close finish. Do not dismiss a
clock warning solely because the race is not dual slalom.

## Reviewing a disputed result

Compare the recorded start, finish, and any corrections with the finish judge's
notes or other timing evidence. Available records can help with review, but the
current timing screen does not provide an expanded audit-history view. See
[History & corrections](/timing/history-and-audit).

Publishing makes results visible; official approval rests with the organizer or
sanctioning body under the event's rules. See the
[timing and results terms](https://time2.bike/legal/terms#results).
