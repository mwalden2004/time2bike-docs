**Live Timing** is how you time a race on Time2Bike. It runs in a normal web
browser. You can record manually without dedicated timing hardware. Set up your races,
bibs, and timing options, then test the devices before race day.

<Frame caption="Demo data.">
  <img src="/images/timing/operator-screen.png" alt="Live Timing" />
</Frame>

## There's nothing to create

Open your event and click the **Live Timing** tab. That tab *is* the timer.

- Your event has **one timer**, made for you the first time you open the tab.
- It covers **every race in the event** automatically.
- Add a race later and the timer picks it up on its own.

The only thing you have to do first is [create your races](/organizers/races/races-list)
and [assign bib numbers](/organizers/events/bibs). If you have no races yet,
the tab tells you so.

<Note>
  You don't pick a timing "mode" either. Each race gets the right screen based
  on the format you already chose when you created it: mass start, time trial,
  downhill, dual slalom. Switch races at the top of the screen and the controls
  change with it. See [Race formats](/timing/formats/mass-start).
</Note>

## What you actually need

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="A phone or laptop" icon="mobile">
    A device and browser you have tested with your timing setup.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Bib numbers on your riders" icon="hashtag">
    Bib numbers are the only rider identity Time2Bike uses. Assign them under
    **Event → Event setup → Bibs & plates**.
  </Card>
</Columns>

A connection is needed to load current event data and share records between
devices. Prepare and test offline recording before relying on it.

## How it works, in one paragraph

Open the Live Timing tab and press **Start** when the race begins. As riders
cross the line you type their bib number and press Enter, or tap their name in
the rider list. Each entry is timestamped when the device processes the recording action. When the race is over you press **Stop**, fix anything that looks wrong,
then **Finalize** and **Publish**, and the results appear on your event page.

## The things that matter on race day

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Prepared devices can record offline" icon="wifi">
    The timer saves actions locally before attempting an upload. With the screen
    and session loaded, you can continue during a connection loss while local
    storage is working. Check the queue and any errors when you reconnect.

    Use **Download for offline** ahead of time and test the same device without
    a connection.

    → [Timing without signal](/timing/hardware/offline-and-sync)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Your whole crew can time at once" icon="users">
    Put four volunteers on four devices and they all record into the same timer.
    Their uploaded actions combine in the same session. Updates require a
    working connection; queued actions and conflicting corrections need review.
    Confirm all devices have uploaded before finalizing.

    Better still, each person says **where on the course they're standing**, and
    the same screen does the right job for each of them.

    → [Recording points](/timing/screen/recording-points)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Volunteers don't need accounts" icon="qrcode">
    You hand out a QR code or a link. Your volunteer scans it and their device
    is a timing device, with no password, no account, and no access to anything
    else in your organization. Switch it off whenever you like.

    → [Volunteer links](/timing/crew/tokens-and-qr)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Correct recordings and use UNDO for recent actions" icon="rotate-left">
    **UNDO** reverses supported recent actions, subject to your role. Result
    controls also let you correct times and statuses using your backup records.
    Resetting timing or losing unsynced device data is different from correcting
    a result and can remove records.

    → [Corrections](/timing/screen/corrections)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Spectators can follow provisional results" icon="radio">
    Flip **Share live results** and you get a public link that updates as you
    record. Live results are provisional. Publishing does not replace the organizer’s
    result-review and protest process.

    → [Sharing live results](/timing/results/live-sharing)
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What it can time

| Your race | Screen you get |
|---|---|
| Mass start — road, XC, gravel, cyclocross, criterium | [Mass start](/timing/formats/mass-start) |
| Time trial, downhill, hill climb, enduro stages | [Interval starts](/timing/formats/interval-starts) |
| Lap racing and criteriums with primes | [Laps & primes](/timing/formats/laps-and-primes) |
| Several races finishing through one chute | [Combined finish](/timing/formats/combined-finish) |
| Dual slalom or 4X | [Dual slalom](/timing/formats/dual-slalom) and [Brackets](/timing/formats/brackets) |
| Relay | [Relay](/timing/formats/relay) |

Staggered starts within a race are handled by [waves](/timing/screen/waves).

## Hardware, if you have it

Everything above works by typing bib numbers. If you already own timing
hardware, you can feed it in instead:

- **RFID pads** — [read from your reader's file](/timing/hardware/rfid), keyed
  by bib.
- **Beam-break gate boxes** — [Time2Bike's laser timing system](/timing/hardware/laser),
  for beam-triggered starts and finishes with box clock-status monitoring.
