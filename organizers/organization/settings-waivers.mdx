Waivers live at the **organization** level so you can author once and attach to any race in any event. **Settings → Waivers**.

![Waivers settings page](/images/orgs/waivers.png)

## Start from a template

**New waiver → Start from** lists releases from sanctioning bodies, with
their rules attached. Picking one fills in the text, the version, who it
applies to, and the statements a signer has to tick — and shows the issuer's
requirements beside the form so none of them is missed.

### US Endurance

The USE Waiver and Release of Liability (specific-event form) is included.
Using it means meeting USE's own conditions for taking it electronically,
and each is handled like this:

| USE requires | How Time2Bike meets it |
| --- | --- |
| The event name and date(s) on the release | Written as `{{event.name}}` and `{{event.dates}}` in the text and filled in from the event every time the release is shown. The signature's content hash covers the filled-in text. |
| Name, address, city/state/zip, phone, email, and the date completed | All required on the online registration form. At the desk, fill in address and phone for a walk-up. Name, email and the signing time are always recorded. |
| Participants select *"I agree to the terms & conditions of the Agreement."* | Asked as a required checkbox at signing, online and at the desk. The exact wording is stored on the signature. |
| For minors, a parent or legal guardian completes the form, the minor's and guardian's names are collected, and the guardian agrees to both guardian statements | A rider under the governing state's age of majority is asked for a guardian's name and relationship, and the two guardian statements replace the rider's one. |
| Kept for a minimum of 7 years | Signatures stay with the registration. Clearing an event's real entries deletes them, and the clear-entries dialog says so. Don't do it for a USE event. |

The text was transcribed from USE's own form. Check with US Endurance for
the current wording before relying on it.

## Create a waiver

1. Click **New waiver**.
2. Set:
   - **Title** — what riders see.
   - **Version** — free-form string (e.g. `2025.1`). Each signed waiver records the exact version the rider agreed to.
   - **Applies to** — **Adults**, **Minors**, or **Everyone**. An adults-only
     release signed by a fourteen-year-old covers nothing, so it isn't offered
     to them. See [Who signs](#who-signs-riders-and-guardians) below for where
     this is enforced.
   - **Content** — markdown body of the waiver. Write `{{event.name}}` and
     `{{event.dates}}` where the event goes; they're filled in per event.
   - **Statements to tick** — one per line, separately for a rider signing
     for themselves and for a parent or guardian. Each becomes its own
     required checkbox at signing, and the wording is stored on the
     signature. Leave blank for a waiver with no such requirement.
3. **Save**.

### Attaching the original PDF

Open a waiver and use **Attach PDF** to keep the issuer's own document
beside it. Riders get a *View the original PDF* link on every signing
screen, and each signature records the PDF's hash.

The markdown text remains the document that is signed, and that's
deliberate. A PDF can't be read to the end on a phone, can't be shown
offline at the desk, can't fill in the event name, and can't be hashed as
what the rider actually saw. The PDF is the source; the text is what's
agreed to.

Once attached to a race, riders sign during checkout (see [Waivers](/riders/registering/waivers)) and the signature, timestamp, IP, and content hash are stored.

## Who signs: riders and guardians

A minor's signature on a liability release is voidable, which makes a wrongly
self-signed release worse than none — it looks like protection and isn't. So
Time2Bike decides who signs from the rider's age:

- Riders at or over the **age of majority** sign for themselves.
- Below it, a parent or guardian signs, and their name and relationship to the
  rider are recorded alongside the signature.

The age of majority is state law rather than a flat 18: it's **19** in Alabama
and Nebraska and **21** in Mississippi and Puerto Rico. Time2Bike applies the
one for the state the waiver is governed in, and everywhere else uses 18.

The state is the one on the waiver where it's set, and otherwise your
organization's mailing address, so most organizers never have to think about it.

A rider with no birthday on file is asked for a guardian, and told why. That's
the safe direction, and the fix is to collect the birthday.

<Note>
  The same rule is applied to league waivers, so the same seventeen-year-old
  signing an event waiver and a league waiver on the same afternoon can't be a
  minor for one and an adult for the other.
</Note>

### Where this is applied today

**Applies to** and the guardian rules govern every signature Time2Bike collects
on a rider's behalf: the [check-in desk](/organizers/events/check-in),
[manual entry](/organizers/registrations/manual-entry), and league waivers. Each
of those shows a rider only the documents that apply to their age, and won't
record a minor's signature without a guardian.

In the rider's own checkout, every waiver attached to the race is still
presented. If you use adults-only and minors-only documents, expect riders
registering online to see both for now.

## Signing at the desk

A rider signing in their own checkout signs for themselves. A walk-up, or a
rider who turns up unsigned, signs at the check-in desk on an official's
tablet — and until now that meant the official typed the rider's name. That
records that an official was present. It doesn't record that the rider agreed
to anything.

**Settings → Waivers → Signing at the check-in desk** changes what the desk
collects. Two choices, saved as you pick them:

| Setting | Options | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Signature** | Typed by the official / Drawn by the rider | *Drawn* hands the tablet across. The rider reads the document to the end, types their name and signs with a finger. |
| **Photo at signing** | No photo / Offered / Asked for | A picture of the signer, taken on the tablet as they sign. *Asked for* still lets the desk skip it, but a reason is recorded. |

**Preview what the rider sees** opens the exact screen a rider would be
handed, using one of your real waivers. Nothing is saved from a preview.

<Tip>
  For walk-ups, use a drawn signature with a photo. A walk-up has no online
  checkout behind them — no account, no card on file, often no date of
  birth — so what the desk records is the only evidence you'll ever have
  that this person agreed to the release.
</Tip>

### What the rider sees

The screen shows the document and nothing else — not the price, not the
rest of the field. The rider scrolls to the end (or says they've read it;
which one is recorded), signs, takes the photo if asked, and taps **Hand
back**. Only then does the desk return. The official sees the signature and
the photo before recording it; that look is what makes them the witness.

For a minor, the screen asks the parent or guardian to sign, and their name
and relationship are recorded — the same rule as everywhere else.

### What is stored

Alongside the usual timestamp, IP and content hash, a desk signature records
**who held the pen** — the rider, a guardian, or the official typing on their
behalf — plus the signature strokes themselves (with timing, which is what
tells a live hand from a pasted image), whether the text was read to the
end, and the photo if one was taken. All of it shows in the registration's
details under **Waiver signing proof**.

<Note>
  The photo is evidence, not identification. Nobody compares it to anything
  at the desk; its value is later, if somebody says they never signed. It is
  stored privately, shown only to people who can see the registration, never
  published, and never run through face recognition.
</Note>

### Nothing here blocks the desk

A camera that won't start, a rider who'd rather not be photographed, a
tablet with no space left — each is recorded as a reason against the
signature, and the signature is still taken. The one thing a desk can't do is
quietly loosen the setting: the record always says what was asked for and
what was actually collected.

## Default waivers

Every race requires at least one waiver, and it's nearly always the same one.
Click **Set default** on a waiver to attach it to every new race you create.

More than one waiver can be a default — a club that needs its own release
alongside a venue's can star both, and both are prefilled.

A default is a starting point, not a constraint. The race create form prefills
your defaults and you can change them there; whatever you pick on the race wins.

→ [Groups & races](/organizers/races/races-list)

## Versioning

Editing a waiver creates a new version. Existing signatures continue to point at the version that was active when they signed — your audit trail is preserved.

Attach waivers to races from the race's **Waivers** tab (see [Race waivers](/organizers/races/race-waivers)).
