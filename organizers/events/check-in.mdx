**Event → Check-in** is the desk on race morning: the twenty minutes where a
roster becomes riders with plates on their bikes. Use it to record arrivals and review registration details.

## What the screen is

One row per rider — not per entry. Somebody in three races is one card with
three races listed on it, so the desk serves a person rather than a spreadsheet.

Across the top: **in**, **part done**, **to come**, and **flagged**. Below that,
the field.

The whole field loads in one request and is filtered in your browser, so the
search box keeps working when the signal doesn't. Load the roster while connected before using it offline.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Search** | Name, bib, or team. Type-ahead, no network |
| **Hide checked in** | Shrinks the list to who's left |
| **Filter roster** | Narrows the screen to one race — for a desk working only the 8am field |
| **Check-in action** | Whether a tap checks a rider into **All shown races** or **one at a time** |
| **Refresh** | Pulls in what the other desk has done, and says how old your list is |

**Filter roster** and **Check-in action** appear on events with more than one race, and
they're separate on purpose: you can have every race on screen and still want to
check somebody in for just one of them.

<Note>
  Waitlisted and unpaid riders are on the list too. Somebody who turns up
  believing they're entered is a conversation that goes better with the truth on
  screen.
</Note>

## Checking someone in

Tap **Check in** beside the rider. A confirmation names the rider and the races
you checked in. Choose **Undo** in that confirmation to reverse the action.

- **One tap covers the races currently shown.** The roster filter also limits
  the races affected by check-in. Choose **One at a time** to use a separate
  control for each race. Team and race details stay visible beneath the rider’s name.
- **Undo keeps the record.** Undoing a check-in marks it undone rather than
  deleting the original check-in record.

## It works offline

This is the screen most likely to be run in a field with no signal, so it's
built for it.

The desk queues check-ins in browser storage and retries them when connected.
It also saves a roster snapshot for offline use. This depends on the browser
being able to save and retain that data.

A bar appears only when there's something to say:

- **Offline** — "check-ins are saved here and sent when the signal returns."
- **Syncing** — how many writes are still queued.

Before leaving the device, reconnect and check that pending writes have
finished syncing. Reopen the same browser to resume queued work. Clearing site
data or losing the device before sync can lose unsent changes. Review any
rejection or storage error shown by the screen.

→ [Offline & sync](/timing/hardware/offline-and-sync)

## Flags

A flag is something worth a second look before a rider starts. There are four:

| Flag | Fires when |
|---|---|
| **Waiver** | A race requires waivers and the entry has no recorded signature or paper-signing resolution |
| **Licence** | Recorded license and fee information does not cover the race date |
| **Unpaid** | The entry is not paid |
| **Minor** | The stored birthday places the rider under 18 on race day |

Two rules keep the colour worth reading.

**One flag per rider, not per entry.** A rider in three races has one licence
problem, not three. The one-day licence fee in particular is charged once per
rider per date and deliberately deduped across their other entries, so the old
per-entry view told somebody twice that they hadn't bought a licence they'd just
bought.

**A flag that fires on everybody isn't news.** When licensing is on but no
licence file was ever imported, every rider is flagged; at a junior league every
rider is under 18. Those are facts about the event, so they're said once in the
header and the badge on each card goes grey — still there, still tappable, no
longer pretending to be new information.

<Warning>
  Flags do not block the **Check in** action. Checking a rider in records their
  arrival; it does not confirm eligibility, waiver validity, or readiness to
  start. Review the event's requirements and the underlying records.
</Warning>

## Fixing a flag at the desk

Tap a flag to review the available actions and record how you addressed it:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Waiver" icon="file-signature">
    **Signed at the desk** — collects signature details for the current version of
    each document that applies to them, stored like any other signature. How
    it's taken depends on your [desk signing
    setting](/organizers/organization/settings-waivers#signing-at-the-desk):
    either the official types the rider's name, or the tablet is **handed to
    the rider**, who reads the document, signs in their own hand, and
    optionally has a photo taken. The tablet comes back showing you the
    signature and photo for your review before you submit them.

    With the roster and waiver text already loaded, desk signing can queue
    offline. Confirm it has synced and review any capture warnings afterwards.

    **Signed on paper** — records the official's report that a paper form was
    signed. Keep the paper form; this action does not create an electronic
    copy of it.

    Document selection uses the stored birthday. When the waiver's configured
    policy requires guardian details, the form requires a guardian's name.

    → [Waivers](/organizers/organization/settings-waivers)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Licence" icon="id-card">
    **Add a licence** — enter the number to save it to the rider's profile.
    USA Cycling numbers use an online lookup. Other issuers currently have
    no live verification; check their requirements separately.

    **Sell a one-day** — cash, card, or waived. It lands on the licence-filing
    list either way; a waived fee is recorded as zero. Confirm issuance and
    filing requirements with the sanctioning body.

    → [Licences & one-day fees](/organizers/events/licenses)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Unpaid" icon="credit-card">
    **Cash**, **card**, or **waived**. Cash and waived mark the entry paid with
    a note about which. Card takes the money — see below.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Minor" icon="user">
    **Guardian present** — records the adult's name and their relationship to
    the rider.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

A resolution applies to the entries covered by the flag. After it syncs,
refresh another station to load the updated record.
Resolved flags stay visible but quiet, labelled with what was done ("Signed at
the desk", "One-day paid cash", "Fee waived").

## Taking money at the desk

Two ways, because a race venue is not an office.

**QR code** — the rider opens Stripe Checkout on their phone. Their card is
entered on that phone. Check the entry's payment status after checkout;
confirmation may arrive after the rider finishes.

**Card on the desk device** — use the card field on the official's tablet.
This needs an internet connection. Review the payment result on screen and
confirm the entry is marked paid before treating the charge as completed.

Card payments use the organization's connected Stripe account. Review the
amount and fee shown before charging. Refunds and payouts follow the applicable
payment flow and account status; see [Stripe Connect](/platform/stripe-connect).

Entry fees and one-day licences are charged separately — a rider who owes only a
licence isn't asked for an entry as well. The organizer's revenue and the money
collected on behalf of the sanctioning body stay distinct.

<Note>
  Taking card payments needs a connected Stripe account on the organization. Cash
  and waived work regardless.

  → [Stripe Connect](/platform/stripe-connect)
</Note>

## Walk-ups

**Walk-up Registration** enters somebody who isn't on the list at all. It's the
other half of what this screen is for, so it's a button rather than something
buried in a menu.

It's the same form as [Manual entry](/organizers/registrations/manual-entry),
running in race-morning mode, and it adds four things:

<Steps>
  <Step title="A price per race, quoted today">
    Every race in the event with what a walk-up into it costs right now, plus
    the group it belongs to — event races reuse names across groups, and a bare
    race name in a picker puts riders on the wrong start line.

    The number comes from the race's day-of rate where one is set, and otherwise
    from whatever price tier resolves today, rather than refusing to quote.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The rules this entry would cross, named">
    Registration closed. The race is full. There are people on the waitlist who
    queued properly and are about to be walked past.

    The form lets an authorized operator acknowledge these registration
    warnings and records that choice. An override does not waive permit,
    capacity, or eligibility requirements set outside Time2Bike.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The licence question, asked now">
    Add an existing licence, record a one-day, or skip the field when you will
    handle the check separately. Review the issuer-specific lookup limits
    described above.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Payment and a plate, without leaving the screen">
    Take the money, then hand over a number. The desk suggests one: the plate
    the rider already resolves to — a series plate they carry all season, or one
    assigned earlier at this event — or the next free number if they have none.

    → [Bibs](/organizers/events/bibs)
  </Step>
</Steps>

Walk-ups are tagged as such on the registration, so when a permit headcount or a
revenue number looks wrong afterwards, they're findable.

### Comping an entry

Where a race allows it, the desk can give an entry away — a volunteer, a
sponsor, the friend who drove the moto. A comp asks for a reason and records
what the entry would have cost, so "what did we give away this season" has an
answer in your records.

Comping is off unless the race allows it; the button says so when it's
unavailable.

## Print the roster

**Print roster** opens **Race day paper** — the sheets a desk actually needs on
a clipboard:

- **Start lists** — who's in each race.
- **Call-up sheet** — the order riders are called to the line.
- **Podium sheet** — blank places for the awards tent.

Pick the races you want — a whole group in one tap — and choose how they're
broken up: split by age group, by gender, or both, each block on its own sheet,
and a new sheet per race so different people can be handed different fields. The
dialog estimates the page count as you go, since saving paper is the point. The
podium sheet also asks how many places to leave room for.
