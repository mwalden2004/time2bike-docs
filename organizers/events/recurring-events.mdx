Most events come back. The same weekend next year, the first Tuesday of every
month, every Wednesday evening through the summer. Time2Bike can treat those
runnings as one **recurring event**, which changes three things:

- **The web address stops changing.** `time2.bike/events/spring-classic` is the
  race, forever. It always shows whichever running is current, so the link on
  your flyer, in your newsletter and on your sponsor's site keeps working — and
  keeps building up search ranking instead of starting over every year.
- **A finished race stops being a dead end.** Someone landing on last season's
  page in February gets the next date, or the chance to be told when there is
  one, instead of "this event has ended".
- **Results collect in one place.** Every running's results, on one page,
  under the race's own address.

<Warning>
  **Create the event first.** A recurring event is always built from one that
  already exists — there is no "new recurring event" button, and there isn't
  meant to be.

  So: make your event and run it (or at least set it up), then open it and
  choose **Schedule the next one**. That is the moment the recurring event comes
  into being, with your existing event as its first running.

  → [Create an event](/organizers/events/create-event)
</Warning>

<Note>
  Nothing about creating an ordinary event changes, and most events never become
  recurring. You are never asked about any of this up front.
</Note>

## Scheduling the next one

Open a race that has already run (or is about to) and go to **Settings → Does
this race happen again? → Schedule the next one**.

{/* IMAGE NEEDED: /organizations/[id]/events/[id]/settings → "Does this race happen again?" panel */}

You get a date, already filled in from the schedule you set — the third Saturday
in May, the first Tuesday of the month — and two lists. If you haven't set one,
the date is blank and you pick it.

| Carries over | Starts empty |
|---|---|
| Race categories and the races inside them | Registrations and the start list |
| Registration questions | Results and timing data |
| Pricing schedules | Bib and plate assignments |
| Waivers | Capacity counts and waitlists |
| Course, distance and format settings | Discount code usage |
| Sponsor policy and event pages | Publication status — the new one is a draft |

The new running is created as a **draft**, linked to the previous one. Nothing
is public until you publish it as usual.

<Warning>
  Registration open and close dates are deliberately **not** copied. They are
  fixed dates, so a copy would either have a window that already closed or —
  worse — one that is already open, taking real entries for a race you are
  still building. Set them on the new running.
</Warning>

## The race's own page

Its permanent address shows, in order:

- your banner, the race's name and where it usually happens;
- **what's next** — the next running with its entry link, a date you've set but
  not built yet, or the schedule you stated;
- **about this race** — what it is, for somebody who has never heard of it;
- **race info** — the pages you've written for the race: rules, course, travel;
- **previous runnings**, grouped by year;
- a signed-in rider's own record here;
- sponsors attached to the race.

Every one of those sections can be turned off. **Recurring events → your event →
Settings → What the public page shows.**

## Editing a recurring event

**Recurring events → your event → Settings** covers all of it: name, web address,
banner, description, where it usually happens, the schedule, the next date, what
shows publicly, and ending it.

<Warning>
  Changing the **web address** breaks every link already shared — flyers, emails,
  your sponsors' sites — and moves where each running redirects. Old addresses
  don't forward. The whole point of a recurring event is that this address never
  has to change, so change it before you publicise anything, or not at all.
</Warning>

## Saying when it runs

Time2Bike does **not** guess your schedule. You state it, and the page repeats
exactly what you said.

Under **Settings → When does it run?**, build it from how often, which days and
which week of the month:

| You set | Your page says |
|---|---|
| Monthly · second · Saturday | Second Saturday of every month |
| Yearly · July · first · Sunday | First Sunday in July |
| Weekly · Wednesday | Every Wednesday |
| Monthly · the 15th | The 15th of every month |

The builder shows the exact sentence as you go, so you never have to publish
something to see how it reads.

If your schedule doesn't fit any of that — "Memorial Day weekend", "the Saturday
before Thanksgiving", "whenever the snow clears" — type it into **Or say it in
your own words**, and that's what appears instead.

<Note>
  Leave the schedule off entirely and the page simply shows the last date the
  race was held. That's a fact, and it's better than a guess. Earlier versions of
  this worked the pattern out from your dates and published it — a race with one
  running announced it was "usually held in August". That's gone; nothing is
  inferred any more.
</Note>

## Adding a running you already made

**Recurring events → your event → Add a running.** Pick any event that isn't
already part of a race.

The grouping suggester only spots what it can match by name and venue, so a race
that was renamed, moved town, or was called something else in 2019 will never be
suggested. Adding it by hand is how those years join their own history.

The event keeps its own web address, entries and results — it just joins the
race.

## Ending a recurring event

Two different things, under **Settings**:

- **No longer run** keeps the page, the address and the results archive, and
  stops the page promising a next date. This is what you want for a race that
  has finished for good.
- **Delete** removes the grouping and the permanent address. Every event it held
  keeps its own address, entries and results — nothing is deleted — but
  `time2.bike/events/your-race` stops working, and the riders waiting to hear
  about the next one are lost with it.

## Riders waiting to hear

When there's no next date announced, and you've left the "Tell me when it's on"
form switched on, riders can leave their email on the race page or on any
finished running's page. You'll see the count on the race in
**Events → Recurring events**, and they're reachable from **Messaging** like any
other audience.

That list is worth taking seriously. Somebody who goes looking for your race
eight months out has already decided to enter it.

## Grouping events you've already run

If you've been running events here for a while, Time2Bike will spot runnings of
the same event and ask about them under **Events → Recurring events**:

> **5 runnings of Spring Classic?** Usually the third Saturday in May.

Accepting groups them, and the whole history appears at once — the archive is
full immediately rather than in five years.

It only ever asks. Nothing is grouped automatically, because a wrong guess would
put one race's history on another race's public page.

<Tip>
  Got it wrong, or accepted a grouping you shouldn't have? Open the race and
  choose **Not this race** beside any running. It's removed from the group and
  nothing else changes — the event keeps its address, entries and results.
</Tip>

## What happens to your existing links

When a race becomes recurring it takes the permanent form of its address:

- If the event's address had a year in it — `spring-classic-2025` — the race
  takes `spring-classic` and the event keeps the address it had.
- If the event's address had no year — `spring-classic` — the race takes it, and
  that event moves to `spring-classic-2025`.

Either way **every link you have already shared keeps working**. In the second
case the link people already have gets better: it now points at the race, so it
shows whichever running is current rather than one frozen year.

Each running also gets an address beneath the race's:
`time2.bike/events/spring-classic/2025`, or
`time2.bike/events/tuesday-tt/2026-03-04` for a race that runs more often than
once a year.

## Series that come back

A series recurs exactly as much as a single race day does — a Winter Series that
runs every year is one recurring event whose every running is a whole season,
rounds and all.

Open the series, go to **Edit details**, and use **Does this series run again? →
Schedule the next season**. Same flow as a single event: you get a date, the two
lists of what carries and what resets, and the new season is created as a draft
linked to this one.

Everything else works the same afterwards — one permanent address at
`time2.bike/series/your-series`, every season listed under it, and a results
archive across all of them.

Season passes are never copied. A pass is a product with a price and a sales
window, and quietly recreating one would put something purchasable on the
internet that you hadn't decided to sell. A pass also never spans seasons — it
covers the rounds of the season it belongs to.

→ [Series](/organizers/series/create-series)

## The results archive

Each running's results are public on its own page either way. What you choose is
whether they're also collected into one archive on the race's page, at
`time2.bike/events/your-race/results`. The switch is on the race, under
**Events → Recurring events**.

The archive shows every running with its finisher count and the winner of each
field, and links through to the full table for any one of them. Riders who are
signed in also see their own record at the race on its main page — how many
times they've done it, their best time, and how many years running.

### Best times, and being honest about them

The archive lists the fastest time on record for each field. If your course has
moved, set a **course version** on the race — a short label like "2019 course"
or "post-bridge" — and times are grouped by it, with a note saying the field has
raced more than one.

<Note>
  Without a course version, the archive says "fastest on record" rather than
  "course record". A record that spans a course change isn't a comparison, and
  it's not a claim worth making on your own results page.
</Note>

## Comparing one running to the next

Open a recurring event and you'll see the two numbers a race director actually
plans against.

**The entry curve** plots entries against how long before race day they came in,
one line per running, overlaid. It's the only way to know whether being 40
entries down six weeks out is a problem or exactly where you were last year.

**Returning against new** counts riders who raced an *earlier* running. A race
with flat entry numbers and a falling returning share is in trouble a year
before the total shows it.

{/* IMAGE NEEDED: /organizations/[id]/recurring-events/[id] → entry curve chart */}

The first running has no returning figure at all — nothing preceded it, so a
percentage there would be a claim about riders who didn't exist yet.

## Letting your regulars in first

For a race that sells out, an early window is worth more than a discount: your
regulars get a place without a lottery.

On any race in the running, set **Early access opens** alongside the usual
**Registration opens** date. Between the two, only riders holding a paid entry
from an earlier running of this race can enter. From the public open date it's
open to everyone.

The race page says which state it's in, so nobody discovers they're too early at
the checkout step.

<Warning>
  Setting early access also makes Time2Bike enforce that race's **Registration
  opens** date, which it otherwise treats as display-only. Set both dates
  deliberately — if you leave the public open date empty, the race is open to
  everybody as soon as the early window starts.
</Warning>

## Sponsors that belong to the race

A bike shop that has backed your race for nine years belongs to the *race*, not
to any one year of it. Set a sponsor's scope to the recurring event and it
appears on every running, including ones you create later — so it can't quietly
get dropped at a rollover.

## Being reminded

When the schedule you set says the next one is about due and nothing is on the
calendar, it appears under **Due for the next one** on the Recurring events page,
along with how many riders are waiting to hear.

The lead time follows the schedule: a few weeks for a weekly series, a few months
for an annual race. A race with no schedule set only shows up here once it has
been silent for over a year. Nothing is ever created for you — a draft you didn't ask for
is just something else to delete.

## Handing one on

Races change hands. Transfer the recurring event to another organization and the
name, the web address, the results archive and the riders waiting to hear all go
with it.

Past runnings **stay with the organization that ran them** — those were their
events, their entries and their payouts, and none of that moves. What the new
owner gets is the race's identity and its history, which is the part that would
otherwise be lost.

## Cloning vs scheduling the next one

Both make a draft copy. The difference is the link:

- **Schedule the next one** — this race, happening again. The runnings are tied
  together, share an address, and build a shared results history.
- **Clone event** — a *different* race that starts from the same setup. No
  connection between the two.
